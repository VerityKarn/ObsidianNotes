# Background/Relevance
Holt's first book, published in 1964, and revised in 1982, which gave him his initial audience and fame. It was followed up by [[How Children Learn by John Caldwell Holt]], which has become his more famous work among homeschooling advocates, and establishes some of the "unschooling" principles that he expanded upon later. Primarily, this book offers criticisms of educational institutions, based around a series of memos and journal entries that Holt made during his short time as an elementary school teacher. Much of this was originally with the goal of reforming educational practices, which he dropped later in his career for advocacy for alternative schooling or "unschooling". While that makes portions of this book irrelevant to later works, there are still themes that are started here which continually pop up, such as children's relationships to adults and authority, or the idea that children are naturally curious and schools destroy that curiosity.

- He uses a phrase, "producer-thinker", that I've seen in another one of his books, but it gets better defined here. Most students, Holt argues, are *producers*, which don't internalize information or systems, but utilize strategies that produce the right answer. In more recent terms, we'd say that these are "test-taking" students, that learn how to take tests, but don't actually retain info. *Thinkers* are those students that actively engage with the material, retaining info as part of a wider mind mapped to the core of the information, often creatively applying knowledge rather than memorizing it.
